# Moghazy University Student Handbook (workshop edition)

Moghazy University does not exist. This handbook was written for a RAG workshop, and every rule, number and course code in it is invented, so a language model cannot know any of it in advance.

## Academic Calendar

**Semesters.** The academic year has two teaching semesters of fourteen weeks each. The autumn semester starts on the second Monday of September and the spring semester starts on the first Monday of February. Each semester ends with a two-week assessment period.

**Reading week.** Week eight of every semester is reading week. No lectures or labs are held, but the library and study rooms keep their normal hours. Coursework deadlines may still fall inside reading week.

**Add and drop period.** Students may add a course during the first seven days of the semester. A course can be dropped with no record on the transcript until the end of week three. A course dropped between week four and week nine appears on the transcript with the letter W.

## Registration

**Credit load.** A full-time student registers for between 24 and 36 credits per semester. Taking more than 36 credits needs written approval from the academic advisor and a cumulative GPA of at least 3.2.

**Academic advisor.** Every student is assigned an academic advisor in their first week. Students must meet their advisor at least once per semester before the registration portal opens. Registration stays locked until the advisor confirms the meeting.

**Attendance.** Attendance is recorded in labs and tutorials but not in lectures. A student who misses more than three lab sessions in one course without an approved reason is not allowed to submit the lab portfolio for that course.

## Courses and Prerequisites

**CS-101 Introduction to Programming.** A first course in Python for students with no coding background. It covers variables, loops, functions and simple file handling. CS-101 has no prerequisites and is offered in both semesters.

**MA-110 Discrete Mathematics.** Logic, sets, proof techniques, counting and graphs. MA-110 has no prerequisites and is normally taken in the first year alongside CS-101.

**CS-204 Data Structures and Algorithms.** Lists, trees, hash tables, graphs, sorting and complexity analysis. Students may not enrol in CS-204 until they have passed both CS-101 and MA-110. It is offered in the autumn semester only.

**CS-240 Databases.** Relational modelling, SQL, transactions and indexing. The only prerequisite for CS-240 is CS-101. It is offered in the spring semester only.

**CS-310 Machine Learning.** Regression, classification, neural networks and model evaluation. Entry requires CS-204 and MA-215 Linear Algebra, each with a grade of C or higher. The course has a limit of 60 places.

**Electives outside the faculty.** Students may take up to 12 credits of electives from another faculty during their degree. These credits count toward graduation but are graded pass or fail and do not affect the GPA.

## Assessment and Grading

**Grade scale.** Courses are graded A, B, C, D or F. The pass mark is 50 percent, which corresponds to grade D. Grade A starts at 85 percent, grade B at 70 percent and grade C at 60 percent.

**Late work.** Coursework submitted after the deadline loses 10 percent of the available marks for each calendar day it is late, up to a maximum of five days. Work submitted more than five days late receives a mark of zero unless an extension was approved in advance by the course coordinator.

**Extensions.** A request for more time must be sent through the student portal at least 48 hours before the deadline, with supporting evidence such as a medical note. The course coordinator replies within two working days. The longest extension that can be granted is ten days.

**Grade appeals.** A student who believes a mark is wrong may ask for a re-mark within 14 days of the result being published. The re-mark fee is 30 dollars and is refunded if the mark goes up. A re-mark can also lower the mark.

**Dean's list.** Students who finish a semester with a GPA of 3.7 or higher on at least 30 credits are placed on the Dean's list. The list is published in the second week of the following semester.

## Examinations

**Exam conduct.** Students must bring their university ID card to every exam. Doors close 30 minutes after the start, and nobody may enter the exam hall after that. Phones and smart watches must be switched off and left in a bag at the front of the hall.

**Resits.** A student who does not pass a final assessment may attempt it again once, during the August resit period. The grade for a repeated attempt is capped at C, and a third attempt is not permitted.

**Absence from an exam.** A student who misses an exam because of illness must send a medical certificate to the registry within five working days. If the absence is accepted, the student sits the paper at the next sitting with no cap on the grade.

**Calculators and notes.** Only the calculator models on the approved list may be used in exams. Open-book exams allow one handwritten A4 sheet unless the course coordinator states otherwise in writing.

## Academic Integrity

**Plagiarism.** Presenting another person's words, code or ideas as your own is plagiarism, whether the source is a classmate, a website or a book. Copying your own earlier work without saying so also counts. All coursework is checked with similarity software.

**Use of AI tools.** Students may use AI assistants to explain concepts and to check grammar. Submitting text or code generated by an AI tool as your own work is treated as plagiarism unless the assignment brief explicitly allows it. When AI help is allowed, it must be declared in a short note.

**Penalties.** A first integrity offence leads to a mark of zero for the piece of work and a written warning. A second offence leads to failing the course. A third offence is sent to the disciplinary board and can lead to suspension for up to two semesters.

## Library

**Borrowing.** Undergraduate students may borrow up to eight books at a time for 21 days. A loan can be renewed twice online unless another reader has reserved the book. Reference books and journals cannot leave the building.

**Overdue fines.** An overdue book costs 2 dollars per day, up to 40 dollars per book. A student who owes more than 50 dollars in fines cannot borrow or renew until the amount is paid.

**Opening hours.** The main library is open from 8:00 to 23:00 on weekdays and from 10:00 to 18:00 at weekends. During the assessment period it stays open 24 hours a day for students with a valid ID card.

**Study rooms.** Group study rooms can be booked online for up to two hours per day. A booking is cancelled if nobody arrives within 15 minutes of the start time. Food is not allowed in study rooms, but drinks with a lid are.

## Housing

**Room allocation.** First-year students are guaranteed a room in university housing if they apply before 15 July. Rooms for later years are allocated by lottery in April. A housing contract runs for the full academic year.

**Quiet hours.** Quiet hours in all residences run from 22:30 to 7:00 on Sunday to Thursday and from midnight to 8:00 on Friday and Saturday. During the assessment period, quiet hours apply for 22 hours a day.

**Visitors.** A resident may host one overnight visitor for at most three nights in any month. The visitor must be signed in at the residence desk before 21:00. The resident is responsible for the behaviour of their visitor.

**Kitchens and cleaning.** Shared kitchens are cleaned by staff once a week. Residents are expected to wash their own dishes on the day they use them. Items left in a shared fridge past the Friday clear-out are thrown away.

**Moving out.** Rooms must be empty and keys returned by noon on the last day of the contract. A room left dirty or damaged is charged against the 200 dollar deposit. The rest of the deposit is returned within 30 days.

## Fees and Financial Aid

**Tuition payment.** Tuition is paid per semester and is due by the end of week two. Students may pay in three instalments for a fee of 15 dollars. A student with unpaid tuition after week six loses access to the registration portal.

**Merit scholarship.** The merit scholarship covers half of the tuition fee. To keep it, a student must complete at least 30 credits per semester and hold a cumulative GPA of 3.4 or higher. A student who falls below this gets one probation semester before the award is withdrawn.

**Hardship fund.** Students facing an unexpected financial problem may apply to the hardship fund for a one-off grant of up to 500 dollars. Applications are reviewed every two weeks, and the decision is sent by email.

**Refunds.** A student who withdraws from the university before the end of week two gets a full tuition refund. Withdrawal during weeks three to six gives a 50 percent refund. No refund is given after week six.

## IT Services

**Accounts and passwords.** Every student gets a university account on the day they enrol. A forgotten password can be reset at the self-service page using the phone number on file, or in person at the IT help desk with an ID card. Passwords expire every 180 days.

**Printing.** Each student receives a print credit of 300 pages per semester. Extra pages cost 0.1 dollars for black and white and 0.5 dollars for colour. Unused credit is not carried over to the next semester.

**Software and laptops.** Students can install the campus software bundle on one personal computer. The IT help desk lends laptops for up to seven days to students whose own device is being repaired.

## Health and Wellbeing

**Counselling.** The counselling service offers up to six free and confidential sessions per academic year. Appointments are booked through the wellbeing page, and a same-day drop-in slot is held open every weekday at 13:00 for students who feel overwhelmed.

**Medical centre.** The campus medical centre is open on weekdays from 9:00 to 17:00. Outside these hours, students should call the campus night line, which is answered by a nurse. Medical notes for coursework are issued only for visits made during the illness.

**Disability support.** Students with a disability or long-term condition can register with the access office to receive adjustments such as extra time in exams or lecture recordings. Registration should be completed by week four so that exam arrangements can be made.

## Student Life

**Clubs and societies.** Any group of at least 12 students can register a new club with the student union. Registered clubs receive a starting budget of 150 dollars and may book rooms for free. Club elections are held every March.

**Sports facilities.** The sports hall and the gym are free for students on weekdays before 16:00. After 16:00 and at weekends, a session costs 3 dollars. The swimming pool needs a separate term pass.

**Student ID card.** The student ID card opens the library gates, residence doors and exam halls. A lost card must be reported to the registry on the same day. A replacement costs 10 dollars and is ready in two working days.
